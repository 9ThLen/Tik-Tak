# C1 review — the data-scaling curve, 2026-08-16

**Verdict: `inconclusive`.** The deciding slope spans both zero and the +0.03
MCID, so the run does not distinguish a corpus-limited model from a saturated
one. The registration named this outcome in advance and called it acceptable
rather than a failure; that paragraph is quoted below, because a limitation
predicted before the numbers arrived is worth more than one conceded after.

**This review was written by the party that implemented C1** — the summariser,
the subset generator and the launcher. It is therefore not the independent
check the S0 and S1 reviews were. The numbers below were recomputed from the
nine runs' `records` by a separate script that reads `PREREGISTERED_C1.md`
rather than `c1_summarise`, and opens `summary.json` only to compare; but a
second party still has to repeat this, and until then the agreement recorded
here is self-agreement.

## The deciding endpoint

Primary: paired `F1_100 − F1_50` bar-phase F1, two-way bootstrap over
development works and training seeds, 2,000 draws, seeds `0..1999`. Deciding
axis: the 77 works that are not Candombe.

| axis | mean | two-way 95% CI | class |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **non-Candombe (77 works, deciding)** | **+0.0166** | **[−0.0143, +0.0485]** | **inconclusive** |
| overall (84 works, reported, does not gate) | +0.0182 | [−0.0118, +0.0490] | inconclusive |

The interval contains +0.03, so by the registered non-overlapping rule it is
neither `material` (lower bound ≥ +0.03) nor `saturated` (upper bound < +0.03).
Per the decision table, a non-Candombe `inconclusive` yields `inconclusive`
whatever Candombe does. `selection_sensitive` is `false`: the overall axis
classifies the same way, so the verdict does not depend on the Candombe choice.

**The seeds disagree in sign.** Per-seed slopes on the deciding axis:

| seed | 17 | 29 | 43 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `F1_100 − F1_50` | +0.0231 | **−0.0055** | +0.0322 |

One of three runs got *worse* with four times the data. This is the whole
reason the two-way bootstrap was registered, and it is visible in the width:

| scheme | CI | width |
|---|---|---:|
| two-way (works × seeds, primary) | [−0.0143, +0.0485] | 0.0628 |
| work-only (S1's scheme, secondary) | [−0.0042, +0.0389] | 0.0431 |

Same point estimate, 46% wider interval. Had C1 reported only S1's scheme it
would still have been inconclusive, but it would have understated how little
three seeds pin down — the seed spread is larger than the effect.

## Shape

`F1_50 − F1_25`, reported so the curve can be told from a step:

|---|---:|---|
| non-Candombe | +0.0279 | [−0.0112, +0.0651] |
| overall | +0.0325 | [−0.0050, +0.0742] |

Levels, macro over works, mean of three seeds:

| fraction | phase F1, all 84 | phase F1, non-Candombe 77 |
|---|---:|---:|
| A0 (untrained) | 0.2910 | — |
| 0.25 | 0.3447 | 0.2982 |
| 0.50 | 0.3772 | 0.3261 |
| 1.00 | 0.3955 | 0.3426 |

The first doubling moves the endpoint about twice as far as the second
(+0.0325 against +0.0182 overall). That is the shape of a curve bending over,
but neither step separates from +0.03, so the bend is described, not
established.

Candombe is the only corpus whose interval excludes zero, and it is the one the

miniature: a positive point estimate inside an interval that spans zero.

## Secondary set — none of these gate

Levels are macro means over 84 works; differences carry two-way intervals.

| metric | A0 | 0.25 | 0.50 | 1.00 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| beat F1 | 0.4898 | 0.5666 | 0.5906 | 0.5932 |
| downbeat F1 | 0.2848 | 0.3368 | 0.3666 | 0.3862 |
| coverage | 0.4991 | 0.5934 | 0.6217 | 0.6116 |
| `usable_strict` | 0.1667 | 0.1627 | 0.1984 | 0.2183 |
| stable exact position | 0.2291 | 0.2797 | 0.3044 | 0.3283 |
| false switches / 5 min | 47.31 | 53.81 | 58.00 | 45.88 |
| wrong episodes / 5 min | 14.85 | 17.43 | 17.93 | 16.61 |

Three of these are worth naming.

**Beat F1 has already saturated.** `1.00 − 0.50` is +0.0026 [−0.0181, +0.0210]
against +0.0239 [−0.0033, +0.0557] for `0.50 − 0.25`. Whatever the last
doubling of data bought, it was not beat detection.

**Churn improves only at the full corpus, and is worse than A0 below it.**
False switches per five minutes: `1.00 − 0.50` = **−12.11 [−26.40, −1.03]**,
the one secondary interval that excludes zero. Against A0 the levels are +6.50
at 0.25, +10.68 at 0.50 and −1.43 at 1.00. A partially trained model is
churnier than no training at all. This is a diagnostic and gates nothing, but
it is the sharpest signal in the run and it argues against shipping a model
trained on a corpus fraction.

**Coverage peaks at 0.50 and falls back**: 0.6217 then 0.6116, difference
−0.0100 [−0.0303, +0.0101]. Not separated from zero; recorded so nobody reads
the level table as monotone.

Two runs stopped early, one at each end of the comparison — 0.25/43 and

not a one-sided artifact, but neither is it balance by design.

`update_confound` is recorded unconditionally as `under_fixed_recipe`: data
volume and number of gradient updates move together across fractions, and no
arrangement of these runs separates them. Seeds reaching the epoch cap were
`{17, 29}` at 0.25, `{17, 29, 43}` at 0.50 and `{29, 43}` at 1.00.

## What this does and does not license

It licenses: reporting that four times the data did not produce a demonstrable
bar-phase gain over half the data, with the interval attached.

It does not license: calling the model data-saturated. `saturated` was a
registered class with a definition — upper bound below +0.03 — and the upper
bound is +0.0485. Nor does it license the opposite reading. An interval this
wide is a statement about three seeds, not about the corpus.

The registration anticipated exactly this:

> This is deliberately the weaker-powered choice, and an `inconclusive` from
> interval width is a registered, acceptable outcome rather than a failure.

Raising the seed count was rejected in the registration because it would break
reuse of the S1 anchor, which supplies only three. That trade was made with
open eyes and it is what the run bought.

## Incidents

The sweep was interrupted twice, both times by the same external cause: the
worktree it was running from, `.claude/worktrees/tik-tak-project-migrated-78f643`,
was reassigned to another branch by a different session. The second time it
took `research/training/beatnet` with it and job 3 failed with `No module named
training.beatnet.run`.

Neither interruption touched the evidence. Checkpoint writes are atomic
(`tempfile` + `os.replace`), the interrupted checkpoint loaded with a verifying
identity digest, completed jobs were skipped by a `job_state` check that
compares arm, seed and fraction rather than the presence of a file, and every
run reports the same commit and a clean tree. Jobs 1–2 ran from the original
worktree and jobs 3–6 from a dedicated one created at the same commit; paths
are operational by the registration's own wording, and `experiment_provenance`
takes the commit from `git rev-parse HEAD`, so identity is unchanged.

Recorded because it is the kind of thing that is invisible afterwards and would
be indistinguishable from tampering if it were not written down.
