# C1 review — the data-scaling curve, 2026-08-16

**Verdict: `inconclusive`.** The deciding slope spans both zero and the +0.03
MCID, so the run does not distinguish a corpus-limited model from a saturated
one. The registration named this outcome in advance and called it acceptable
rather than a failure; that paragraph is quoted below, because a limitation
predicted before the numbers arrived is worth more than one conceded after.

**This review was written by the party that implemented C1** — the summariser,
the subset generator and the launcher — so nothing in it counts as independent
acceptance. That came separately, on 2026-08-20, and is recorded in
[`C1_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_20260820.md`](C1_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_20260820.md); it
confirmed the verdict and found four defects in this document, three of them in
prose corrected below. Read this file for the reasoning and that one for the
verification.

## The deciding endpoint

Primary: paired `F1_100 − F1_50` bar-phase F1, two-way bootstrap over
development works and training seeds, 2,000 draws, seeds `0..1999`. Deciding
axis: the 77 works that are not Candombe.

| axis | mean | two-way 95% CI | class |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **non-Candombe (77 works, deciding)** | **+0.0166** | **[−0.0143, +0.0485]** | **inconclusive** |
| overall (84 works, reported, does not gate) | +0.0182 | [−0.0118, +0.0490] | inconclusive |

The interval contains +0.03, so by the registered non-overlapping rule it is
neither `material` (lower bound ≥ +0.03) nor `saturated` (upper bound < +0.03).
Per the decision table, a non-Candombe `inconclusive` yields `inconclusive`
whatever Candombe does.

Separately, the overall axis classifies the same way, so the verdict does not
turn on the Candombe choice. That is a robustness observation about the deciding
axis and **not** the `selection_sensitive` flag, which is about checkpoint
choice and is reported in its own section below. The first draft of this review
conflated the two and asserted the flag on the strength of the Candombe
comparison; the flag's value was right and the reason given for it was wrong.

## Checkpoint choice — the `selection_sensitive` override

Registered: if the deciding slope's selected-checkpoint endpoint and its
last-common-epoch endpoint fall in **different** MCID classes, the outcome is
`selection_sensitive/inconclusive` regardless of the decision table. The
last-common-epoch is taken per seed — the latest epoch all three fractions
evaluated at that seed — because patience truncates the fractions unequally.

| seed | 0.25 points | 0.50 points | 1.00 points | shared epoch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 17 | 10 | 10 | 9 | **45** |
| 29 | 10 | 10 | 10 | **50** |
| 43 | 10 | 10 | 10 | **50** |

| endpoint (non-Candombe, 77 works) | mean | CI | class |
|---|---:|---|---|
| selected checkpoint | +0.0166 | [−0.0143, +0.0485] | inconclusive |
| last common epoch | +0.0228 | [−0.0042, +0.0522] | inconclusive |

Same class, so **`selection_sensitive` is `false`** and the override does not
fire. Note this is the flag not firing, not the endpoints agreeing: the
last-common-epoch estimate is 37% larger and its lower bound is four times
closer to zero. Both land in the same class only because the class is the wide
one.

**The seeds disagree in sign.** Per-seed slopes on the deciding axis:

| seed | 17 | 29 | 43 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `F1_100 − F1_50` | +0.0231 | **−0.0055** | +0.0322 |

One of three runs got *worse* with the extra data. This is the whole
reason the two-way bootstrap was registered, and it is visible in the width:

| scheme | CI | width |
|---|---|---:|
| two-way (works × seeds, primary) | [−0.0143, +0.0485] | 0.0628 |
| work-only (S1's scheme, secondary) | [−0.0042, +0.0389] | 0.0431 |

Same point estimate, 46% wider interval. Had C1 reported only S1's scheme it
would still have been inconclusive, but it would have understated how little
three seeds pin down — the seed spread is larger than the effect.

## Secondary set — none of these gate

Levels are macro means over 84 works; differences carry two-way intervals.

| metric | A0 | 0.25 | 0.50 | 1.00 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| beat F1 | 0.4898 | 0.5666 | 0.5906 | 0.5932 |
| downbeat F1 | 0.2848 | 0.3368 | 0.3666 | 0.3862 |
| coverage | 0.4991 | 0.5934 | 0.6217 | 0.6116 |
| `usable_strict` | 0.1667 | 0.1627 | 0.1984 | 0.2183 |
| stable exact position | 0.2291 | 0.2797 | 0.3044 | 0.3283 |
| false switches / 5 min | 47.31 | 53.81 | 58.00 | 45.88 |
| wrong episodes / 5 min | 14.85 | 17.43 | 17.93 | 16.61 |

Three of these are worth naming.

**Beat F1 shows no detectable gain from the last doubling.** `1.00 − 0.50` is
+0.0026 [−0.0181, +0.0210] against +0.0239 [−0.0033, +0.0557] for
`0.50 − 0.25`. An earlier draft called this "saturated", which borrows a
registered word for an unregistered quantity: +0.03 was registered as a
bar-phase MCID and there is no beat-specific threshold, so the only
supportable statement is that no increase was detected here.

**Churn improves at the full corpus.** False switches per five minutes:
`1.00 − 0.50` = **−12.11 [−26.40, −1.03]**, the one secondary interval that
excludes zero.

An earlier draft went further and said a partially trained model is churnier
than no training at all, from the point differences against A0 — +6.50 at
0.25, +10.68 at 0.50, −1.43 at 1.00. `summary.json` reports those as scalars
with no interval, and the claim was made without computing one. Recomputed
with the same two-way draw, every fraction's contrast against A0 spans zero:
+6.50 [−7.91, +19.96], +10.68 [−4.05, +24.74] and −1.43 [−13.04, +7.45].
The claim is withdrawn. `wrong_episodes_per_5min` at 0.50 does exclude zero
(+3.08 [+0.36, +6.19]), but that is a different metric, one cell of nine, and
unregistered — it is recorded, not promoted.

**Coverage peaks at 0.50 and falls back**: 0.6217 then 0.6116, difference
−0.0100 [−0.0303, +0.0101]. Not separated from zero; recorded so nobody reads
the level table as monotone.

Two runs stopped early, one at each end of the comparison — 0.25/43 and

`update_confound` is recorded unconditionally as `under_fixed_recipe`: data
volume and number of gradient updates move together across fractions, and no
arrangement of these runs separates them. Seeds reaching the epoch cap were
`{17, 29}` at 0.25, `{17, 29, 43}` at 0.50 and `{29, 43}` at 1.00.

## What this does and does not license

It licenses: reporting that doubling the corpus from half to full did not
produce a demonstrable bar-phase gain, with the interval attached. The step
is 330 works against 166 and 10,566,912 frames against 5,883,501 — about 2×
and 1.80×. An earlier draft twice called it four times the data, which is the
100%-against-25% ratio and not the endpoint measured.

It does not license: calling the model data-saturated. `saturated` was a
registered class with a definition — upper bound below +0.03 — and the upper
bound is +0.0485. Nor does it license the opposite reading. An interval this
wide is a statement about three seeds, not about the corpus.

The registration anticipated exactly this:

> This is deliberately the weaker-powered choice, and an `inconclusive` from
> interval width is a registered, acceptable outcome rather than a failure.

Raising the seed count was rejected in the registration because it would break
reuse of the S1 anchor, which supplies only three. That trade was made with
open eyes and it is what the run bought.

## Incidents

The sweep was interrupted twice, both times by the same external cause: the
worktree it was running from, `.claude/worktrees/tik-tak-project-migrated-78f643`,
was reassigned to another branch by a different session. The second time it
took `research/training/beatnet` with it and job 3 failed with `No module named
training.beatnet.run`.

Neither interruption touched the evidence. Checkpoint writes are atomic
(`tempfile` + `os.replace`), the interrupted checkpoint loaded with a verifying
identity digest, completed jobs were skipped by a `job_state` check that
compares arm, seed and fraction rather than the presence of a file, and every
run reports the same commit and a clean tree. Jobs 1–2 ran from the original
worktree and jobs 3–6 from a dedicated one created at the same commit; paths
are operational by the registration's own wording, and `experiment_provenance`
takes the commit from `git rev-parse HEAD`, so identity is unchanged.

Recorded because it is the kind of thing that is invisible afterwards and would
be indistinguishable from tampering if it were not written down.

## Review history

**First external review, 2026-08-16.** Confirmed the endpoint definition, the
verdict rule, the anchor commit and epochs, and that `c1_summarise` implements
the registered draw. Raised two findings:

- **The `selection_sensitive` justification was wrong**, and the
  last-common-epoch endpoint — registered as required reporting — was missing
  entirely. Upheld. Corrected above: the endpoint is now reported with its
  interval and per-seed shared epochs, and the flag is derived from the class
  comparison the registration actually specifies. The flag's value is unchanged.
- **The recomputation could not be repeated** because the raw runs were not in
  that environment. Upheld, and it was the more serious of the two: it meant the
  check could only confirm internal consistency. Addressed by committing
  `C1_RECORDS_20260816.json`; the headline numbers were re-derived from that
  file alone, with no artifact directory and no `summary.json`, and reproduce
  exactly.

**Second external review, 2026-08-16.** Recomputed the deciding endpoints from
the committed bundle with independent NumPy code following the registered draw,
without `summary.json` and without `c1_summarise`. The primary
(+0.0166 [−0.0143, +0.0485]), the last-common-epoch
(+0.0228 [−0.0042, +0.0522]), the work-only secondary ([−0.0042, +0.0389]) and
the three per-seed slopes all reproduced exactly, and both findings were
recorded as closed.

**Third external review, 2026-08-20 — the accepting one.** Recorded in
[`C1_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_20260820.md`](C1_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW_20260820.md).
It authenticated all nine `result.json` files and all eighteen evaluations
the two endpoints use, rebuilt the registered draw without importing any
TikTak code, and opened `summary.json` only afterwards: 155 numeric fields
compared, maximum absolute difference `7.99e-15`. It notes honestly that
the reviewer had seen the headline values in conversation, so the check is
computationally independent but not blinded. **C1's verdict is
independently accepted: valid but inconclusive.**

It also found four defects in this document, none of which touch the
artifact or the verdict. Three were wording and are corrected above, with
the corrections left visible rather than silently applied: the 100%−50%
step twice called "four times the data" when it is about 2× works and
1.80× frames; "churnier than no training at all" asserted from point
differences whose intervals all span zero; and "beat F1 has already
saturated" borrowing a registered word for a metric that has no registered
threshold. Two of the three are the same mistake in different clothes —
stating a comparison as established without computing its interval — which
is precisely what the primary endpoint of this run exists to avoid.

The fourth is a metadata defect in the artifacts rather than the prose. All
six C1 `result.json` files carry `provenance.experiment: "S1"`, because
C1 reuses S1's runner unchanged and the label is fixed there. Identity,
subset digest, fraction, commit and output paths are all correct and the
`identity.c1` block distinguishes the runs, so nothing is ambiguous and the
run stands. But an artifact that names the wrong experiment is a provenance
claim that happens to be harmless here and would not be elsewhere; the
launcher should label what it actually runs before the next curve.

## What still has to happen

1. **A decision on the next step**, which this run does not make. It neither
   promotes more data nor closes that line. The accepting review's own
   consequence: a stronger curve must be registered *before* more training
   output exists, must measure subset-sampling variance with crossed draws,
   must add enough independent seeds to make a +0.03 decision informative,
   and should have its power simulated from this run's seed/work matrix
   rather than assuming three more seeds will resolve the interval.
2. **`provenance.experiment` must name the running experiment** before the
   next curve; see the third review above.
3. **The older runs are not portable, and two are not reproducible.** See the
   note in [`README.md`](README.md); it is not a C1 finding but it was found
   while closing one.
