# S1 stateful block-training ablation — repository-side review

## Overall assessment

S1's registered interpretation is **`stateful_training_negative`**, and it
reproduces exactly. Carrying BeatNet's recurrent state across contiguous TBPTT
blocks does not improve product-level bar phase over resetting it: the paired
work-level difference is **−0.0045 [−0.0194, +0.0102]**, straddling zero against
a registered +0.03 gate.

**The verdict name is narrower than it reads.** S1 compared two trained arms with
each other, and on its own question the answer is a null. The registered A0
diagnostics — added on review precisely because the four verdict names cannot
express this — say something the verdict cannot:

> Both trained arms beat the frozen published model by about **+0.10 of
> bar-phase F1**, with lower bounds well clear of zero.

So training this arm works and carrying state across blocks adds nothing to it.
Those are two separate findings and only the second one is what S1 registered.

One metric does separate the arms, and it is not in the primary endpoint.

## The one place the arms differ

**False switches: −7.00 [−11.23, −3.36] per five minutes**, the only effect in
the run whose interval clears zero. The absolute levels say what it is:

- the untrained model runs at 47.3 false switches per five minutes;
- training with a **reset** at every block boundary raises that to 52.9 — worse
  than not training at all;
- training with the state **carried** leaves it at 45.9.

Read that way, carrying state does not add bar-phase accuracy; it prevents an
operational regression that block-reset training introduces. That is a mechanism
worth recording, and S1 was not designed to test it: the false-switch condition
is registered as a one-sided ceiling on an *increase*, so a large decrease
passes the gate without the finding ever entering the verdict.

`usable_strict` points the same way — 0.167 → 0.206 (reset) → 0.218 (stateful) —
but it is a reported diagnostic with no registered interval here, so it is
weaker evidence than the false-switch interval.

## What the three failed safety gates do and do not say

| gate | value | registered bound | result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| beat F1 | lower −0.0111 | ≥ −0.01 | fails by 0.0011 |
| downbeat F1 | lower −0.0202 | ≥ −0.01 | fails |
| ≥1-bar wrong episodes | upper +1.6761 | ≤ +0.25 | fails |
| stable exact position | lower −0.0230 | ≥ −0.03 | passes |
| false switches | upper −3.3596 | ≤ +1.0 | passes |

**All three failing intervals contain zero.** They fail as "the difference is not
demonstrably inside the tolerance", not as "stateful is worse". Nothing here
shows the candidate harming beat, downbeat or episode behaviour; it shows that
84 works do not resolve a null to within one point.

That is a property of the gate design rather than of the run, and it is worth
carrying forward: these tolerances were sized for a candidate with a real
effect, whose cost then needed bounding. Applied to a candidate with no effect, a
two-sided interval at this sample size fails them by construction. A future
registration that expects a possible null should either size the tolerance to
the interval width it can afford, or state that a straddling interval is an
inconclusive component rather than a failed one.

## Selection did not do the work

The concern raised on review — that the arm surviving longer gets more
maximisation attempts on the same works that carry the endpoint — did not
materialise:

| arm | validation points | eligible | selected epoch |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A3_reset` | 10, 10, 9 | 10, 10, 9 | 30, 30, 25 |
| `A3_stateful` | 9, 10, 10 | 9, 10, 10 | 25, 40, 35 |

Opportunity is nearly balanced, and the non-gating last-common-epoch endpoint —
computed at epochs 45, 50 and 45, with no maximisation at all — gives
**−0.0077 [−0.0252, +0.0080]** against the primary's −0.0045. The two differ by
0.0032, an order below the gate, and both straddle zero. Selection contributed
essentially nothing and the conclusion does not depend on it.

## Registered limitations, unchanged

- These are development corpora the model has already been diagnosed on. S1 is
  an ablation, not an out-of-sample or locked claim, and the A0 gain above
  inherits that limit in full.
- A3 freezes the convolution, `linear0` and LSTM layer 0. A null for A3 does not
  close the question for A4, which the registration keeps behind a separate
  escalation.
- The result bounds the value of carried state **in this training procedure at
  this scale**: three seeds, 400-frame blocks, 100-frame shared warm-up mask,
  50 epochs. A different block length or a longer schedule is not covered.
- Every cache, checkpoint and exported model remains `research_only` under
  RWC 2.0's CC BY-NC 4.0 terms.
