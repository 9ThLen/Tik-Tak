# S1 stateful block-training ablation — repository-side review

## Overall assessment

S1's registered interpretation is **`stateful_training_negative`**, and it
reproduces exactly. Carrying BeatNet's recurrent state across contiguous TBPTT
blocks does not improve product-level bar phase over resetting it: the paired
work-level difference is **−0.0045 [−0.0194, +0.0102]**, straddling zero against
a registered +0.03 gate.

**The verdict name is narrower than it reads.** S1 compared two trained arms with
each other, and on its own question the answer is a null. The registered A0
diagnostics — added on review precisely because the four verdict names cannot
express this — record a second, separate result: both trained arms beat the
frozen published model by about +0.10 of bar-phase F1.

**That +0.10 is not a general improvement, and the pooled number hides what it
is.** Broken down by corpus, 72.4% of it comes from the seven Candombe works,
where the published model scores 0.023 and the trained arms score 0.970.
Excluding Candombe the gain is +0.033 [+0.001, +0.068], and on RWC2 — 63 of the
84 works, and the material closest to the product — it is +0.038 [−0.001,
+0.077], straddling zero. The defensible statement is that **A3 adapts strongly
to a genre the frozen model could not track at all, and moves the rest a little
or not measurably.** Checkpoints were also selected on these same 84 works, so
the A0 intervals carry selection optimism that the paired stateful-vs-reset
endpoint does not.

One metric does separate the arms, and it is not in the primary endpoint.

Seven works of 84 carry 72.4% of the pooled gain. The frozen model cannot track
Candombe at all — 0.023 is chance-level — and after training only LSTM layer 1
and the output layer on 28 Candombe training works it reaches 0.970. That is a
strong statement about where the capacity was missing: the frozen convolution
and LSTM layer 0 already carry enough of candombe's structure, and only the
readout was wrong. It is not a statement about the model getting better in
general, and on the largest and most product-like sub-corpus the interval
contains zero.

## The one place the arms differ

**False switches: −7.00 [−11.23, −3.36] per five minutes**, the only effect in
the run whose interval clears zero. Stateful training produces materially less
bar-state churn than block-reset training.

**What the same numbers do not support is a claim about A0.** The absolute levels
invite one — 47.3 untrained, 52.9 reset, 45.9 stateful — but paired against the
frozen model neither arm separates from it:

| | vs A0, per five minutes |
|---|---:|
| `A3_reset` − A0 | +5.57 [−4.45, +14.26] |
| `A3_stateful` − A0 | −1.43 [−13.03, +7.68] |

Both straddle zero. So "reset training regresses churn and carrying state fixes
it" is not established; only "stateful churns less than reset" is. The paired
arm difference is tight because the arms share seeds and works, and the A0
comparison is not because it adds the between-run spread.

That effect is real and is outside the registered question — the false-switch
condition is a one-sided ceiling on an *increase*, so a large decrease passes
the gate without ever entering the verdict. It deserves its own preregistered
confirmation rather than being carried forward on this run's authority.

`usable_strict` points the same way — 0.167 → 0.206 (reset) → 0.218 (stateful) —
but it has no registered interval here and is weaker evidence still.

## What the three failed safety gates do and do not say

| gate | value | registered bound | result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| beat F1 | lower −0.0111 | ≥ −0.01 | fails by 0.0011 |
| downbeat F1 | lower −0.0202 | ≥ −0.01 | fails |
| ≥1-bar wrong episodes | upper +1.6761 | ≤ +0.25 | fails |
| stable exact position | lower −0.0230 | ≥ −0.03 | passes |
| false switches | upper −3.3596 | ≤ +1.0 | passes |

**All three failing intervals contain zero.** They fail as "the difference is not
demonstrably inside the tolerance", not as "stateful is worse". Nothing here
shows the candidate harming beat, downbeat or episode behaviour; it shows that
84 works do not resolve a null to within one point.

This is not a mathematical inevitability — it is what these particular
tolerances produced against the variance actually observed at 84 works, and a
tighter-varying metric would have passed. But it is worth carrying forward:
these tolerances were sized for a candidate with a real effect whose cost then
needed bounding, and a null candidate is a case they were not written for. A
future registration that thinks a null is plausible should either size the
tolerance to the interval width it can afford at its sample size, or say in
advance that a straddling interval is an inconclusive component rather than a
failed one.

## Selection did not do the work

The concern raised on review — that the arm surviving longer gets more
maximisation attempts on the same works that carry the endpoint — did not
materialise:

| arm | validation points | eligible | selected epoch |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A3_reset` | 10, 10, 9 | 10, 10, 9 | 30, 30, 25 |
| `A3_stateful` | 9, 10, 10 | 9, 10, 10 | 25, 40, 35 |

Opportunity is nearly balanced, and the non-gating last-common-epoch endpoint —
computed at epochs 45, 50 and 45, with no maximisation at all — gives
**−0.0077 [−0.0252, +0.0080]** against the primary's −0.0045. The two differ by
0.0032, an order below the gate, and both straddle zero. Selection contributed
essentially nothing and the conclusion does not depend on it.

## Registered limitations, unchanged

- These are development corpora the model has already been diagnosed on. S1 is
  an ablation, not an out-of-sample or locked claim, and the A0 gain above
  inherits that limit in full.
- A3 freezes the convolution, `linear0` and LSTM layer 0. A null for A3 does not
  close the question for A4, which the registration keeps behind a separate
  escalation.
- The result bounds the value of carried state **in this training procedure at
  this scale**: three seeds, 400-frame blocks, 100-frame shared warm-up mask,
  50 epochs. A different block length or a longer schedule is not covered.
- Every cache, checkpoint and exported model remains `research_only` under
  RWC 2.0's CC BY-NC 4.0 terms.
