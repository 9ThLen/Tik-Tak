# M0b time-varying oracle-ladder result — repository-side review

## Overall assessment

M0b is **inconclusive** under the rules fixed in
`research/eval/PREREGISTERED_M0b.md`. This is a valid result, not a failed run:
the input, coverage and sensitivity gates pass, and the current decoder retains
reference phase and grouping very well on static passages. The unresolved
failure is rapid meter-change acquisition. A1 acquires only 0.0837 of changes
within two bars against the registered 0.80 requirement.

The result therefore does **not** open S2 or a metrical adapter. It also does
not establish the registered hard-negative decoder-bottleneck band, because
A1 phase and grouping remain far above its upper-CI boundary. A focused
change-acquisition diagnostic is required before choosing between decoder
work and additional metrical evidence.

## The failure is reacquisition speed, not static retention

There are 39 primary works with at least one annotated grouping change. A1
eventually reacquires 55.4% of changes, but only 8.4% within two bars. At the
work level, 34 of 39 works have at least one eventual acquisition and only 6
of 39 have any acquisition within two bars.

The failure is concentrated in the largest change-bearing corpus:

| corpus | change works | A1 within two bars |
|---|---:|---:|
| BPSD | 2 | 0.5313 |
| Kraisler | 1 | 0.0000 |
| Rubato | 2 | 0.4143 |
| RWC2 | 34 | **0.0404** |

Candombe has no annotated grouping changes in this selection. The small BPSD,
Kraisler and Rubato change supports make their rates diagnostic only. RWC2
drives the registered aggregate and is the right first target for a focused
transition audit.

This distinction matters: the A1 static phase F1 of 0.980 does not show that
the decoder handles changes. It shows that once a grouping/phase is held, the
reference evidence survives. The change endpoint says it generally does not
establish the new state within the product-relevant latency budget.

Thus the exclusions cannot produce either registered decisive band. They

## Evaluation-coverage audit

**Score: 94/100 — NEEDS WORK. Critical gaps: 1.** This score describes whether
the evidence is sufficient to choose the next metrical intervention, not
whether the completed run is valid.

The run is strong on provenance, deterministic statistics, arm parity,
coverage accounting and positive/negative sensitivity controls. The critical
gap is explanatory: records retain aggregate change counts and acquired
latencies in seconds, but not a per-transition trace with old/new grouping,
change time, acquisition time, latency in tactus units/bars and the decoder's
state immediately before and after the boundary. M0b can therefore reject
fast reacquisition without localising why it fails.

Before S2 or a metrical adapter is authorised:

1. preregister a narrow change-acquisition diagnostic, beginning with the 34
   RWC2 change works;
2. persist one row per transition with old/new grouping, reference boundary,
   first valid position-1 decision, latency in seconds/tactus/bars and the
   competing decoder state;
3. repair or fail earlier on duplicate canonical times for the *next* run;
   do not alter or reinterpret this M0b artifact;
4. rerun only the transition diagnostic needed to distinguish decoder inertia,
   missing transition evidence and scorer/acquisition semantics.

S0's positive result and the case for trying S1 remain intact. What stays
blocked is the M0b-gated S2/metrical-adapter branch.

## Annotation-only follow-up before M0c

The fixed RWC2 annotations expose an additional limitation without another
model run. The 34 RWC2 change works contain 123 transitions. All have at least
one complete new-grouping bar, but 56 have only one and 6 have only two.

The registered acquisition time is the start of a complete correct bar. To
observe an acquisition whose start is exactly two bars after the change, a
third complete bar must exist. Therefore only **61/123** RWC2 transitions fully
observe the registered `latency <= 2 bars` endpoint; the other 62 are
right-censored before its boundary. M0b correctly scored them as failures under
its fixed intention-to-treat rule, so its `inconclusive` verdict is unchanged.
What changes is the causal reading: the 0.0404 RWC2 rate cannot by itself be
described as 96% observed decoder failure at two bars.

`PREREGISTERED_M0c.md` fixes the follow-up before replay output: reproduce A1,
persist one trace per transition, report first-bar acquisition on all 123, and
separate the 61 fully observable transitions from right-censored ones before
classifying stale state versus phase/sequence instability.
